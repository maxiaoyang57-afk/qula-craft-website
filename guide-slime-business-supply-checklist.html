Buyer Guide

The Slime Business Supply Checklist: What to Stock Before Launch

Updated July 24, 2026 · by the Qula Craft sales team

Quick answer: a slime business runs on two lists — base supplies you buy locally (glue, activator, scent) and the add-ins that make each jar sellable: charms, clay sprinkles, bingsu beads, glitter and fillers. This checklist covers the add-in half: what to stock, in which pack sizes, and which paperwork retail channels ask for.

The two halves of a slime supply list

New slime sellers often try to source everything from one place and stall. The faster route is splitting the list by where each item is actually cheapest and safest to buy:

Buy locally / anywhereBuy from a craft-supply factory
Glue and activator — heavy, regulated as chemicals, cheap locallyThemed charm and sprinkle packs that define each slime
Scent oils and colorantBingsu beads, fishbowl beads, glitter sequins for texture
Shipping cartons for your outbound ordersResin collector charms and clay slices as toppers
Labels printed to your local rulesJars, PET bottles, OPP bags and private-label packaging

The left column never crosses an ocean well. The right column is exactly what a Yiwu factory line produces — and consolidating it into one order means one QC pass and one shipment instead of five.

Build the add-in matrix by texture, not by picture

Buyers who pick add-ins by photo end up with pretty jars that all feel the same. Slime customers re-order by texture, so stock at least one line per texture family:

Texture familyAdd-inWhere it lives
CrunchBingsu beads (500g bulk bags), fishbowl beadsGlitter & fillers line
ToppingPolymer clay slices — fruit, floral, holiday shapesClay sprinkles line
Collector pieceOne resin charm per jar as the “prize”Resin charms line
SparklePET glitter sequins, gram-packedGlitter & fillers line

Trial math: 30g to 1000g without dead stock

The most expensive mistake in a first order is bulk-buying a theme nobody wants. Our slime packs are built to make the trial cheap: every theme starts at a 30g trial bag and scales to 500g and 1000g bulk of the same recipe, and stock items sell from 1 bag with no minimum-order barrier. So the launch sequence looks like:

  • Round 1 — trial: 30g bags across 5–8 themes (mix holidays and evergreen; the seasonal calendar maps order-deadline months per holiday).
  • Round 2 — commit: re-order only the 2–3 themes that sold, in 500g or 1000g.
  • Round 3 — differentiate: once a theme proves out, move it to a custom mix or your own label so competitors can't stock the identical jar.

Know what your add-ins are made of

Three materials cover almost every add-in on a slime supply list, and they behave differently in a wet base:

  • Polymer clay — PVC-based, cured at roughly 129–135 °C, patterns built as canes so they run through the piece. Arrives fully hardened; used as surface topping.
  • Cast resin — moulded in flexible moulds that yield about 25 to 100 castings before detail softens. Takes the sharpest detail; used for focal charms.
  • PET — resin identification code 1, practically insoluble in water. This is what bingsu beads are, and why they keep their shape in a wet base instead of going mushy.

Knowing which is which saves you from the two classic mistakes: mixing a surface topping through the base where nobody sees the detail, and buying a filler that softens or bleeds after a week on a shelf.

Packaging decides whether you look like a brand

A zip bag says hobby; a labeled jar says brand. Packaging options run from OPP bags to PET bottles, jars and compartment boxes — the packaging comparison guide walks through cost and shelf logic, and the private-label guide covers putting your own name on the line from day one.

The paperwork retail buyers will ask for

Slime add-ins are decorative and non-edible, and the label has to say so — especially for anything shaped like food. If your jars target kids channels or marketplaces, buyers commonly ask for EN 71, ASTM F963, CPC or CPSC-CH-E1002 paperwork; REACH applies for EU-bound orders. Batch test reports are available on request — the quality & safety page lists the full set, and the safety labeling guide has copy-paste warning wording.

Everything in this checklist is decorative craft material, not food and not a toy unless tested as one — label it that way and keep the batch report for the theme you scale.

FAQ

Slime Business Sourcing FAQ

How many themes should a new slime shop stock at launch?

Trial 5–8 themes in 30g bags first, then scale only the 2–3 that sell into 500g or 1000g bulk of the same recipe. Every pack in our slime line is a live production item, so the trial bag and the bulk bag come off the same line.

Do slime add-ins need safety testing before retail?

All add-ins are decorative and non-edible and must be labeled that way. If your kits target kids channels, retailers commonly ask for EN 71, ASTM F963 or CPC paperwork — batch test reports are available on request, and the safety labeling guide covers the wording.

Can one supplier cover charms, bingsu beads and packaging together?

Yes. Our nine lines span clay slices, resin charms, bingsu and fishbowl beads, glitter sequins and private-label packaging — so one consolidated order covers the add-in half of a slime supply list.

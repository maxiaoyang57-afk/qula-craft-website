Best Packaging for DIY Craft Supplies: Bags, Bottles, Jars & Boxes

Updated July 6, 2026 · by the Qula Craft sales team

Quick answer: Choose packaging by channel, not by looks. Bags win on cost and freight, bottles and jars win on shelf, compartment boxes win for kits, gift boxes win for holidays. The cost gap between the cheapest and the premium option is roughly 20–40× per unit — decide before you quote the product.

2. Freight math most first-time buyers miss

Couriers and forwarders charge by volumetric weight when it exceeds actual weight (commonly length×width×height in cm ÷ 5000–6000 per kg). Craft supplies are light; packaging is what fills cubic meters. The same 100kg of product can be a compact bag shipment or a bottle shipment taking triple the carton count. Ask for the carton dimensions and units-per-carton with every quote — you need both to estimate landed cost honestly.

3. Scenario recommendations

  • Online refill / Etsy supplies shop: labeled bags; upgrade bestsellers to jars later.
  • Nail supply distribution: 10g PET bottles in 12-count display boxes.
  • Slime brand: wide-mouth jars for toppings, bags for your in-house production.
  • DIY kit / subscription box: compartment box — one SKU, 8–12 themes inside, highest perceived value per gram.
  • Q4 gifting: printed gift box with insert; plan artwork by the seasonal deadlines · Slime business starter supply checklist.

3b. Pack format is a freight decision too

Craft supplies are light and bulky, so air and courier shipments are almost always billed on volumetric weight — a figure derived from carton dimensions rather than actual kilos. A rigid jar protects the product and looks premium on a shelf, but it ships air inside it. A flat bag of the same contents can cut the cubic volume substantially.

This is also why the shipping term on your quotation matters: the ICC's Incoterms® rules — first published in 1936, current edition Incoterms® 2020 — determine whether that freight cost sits with you or is already inside the price you were quoted. Ask for carton dimensions and units per carton alongside the unit price, or the two quotes you are comparing are not comparable.

A practical middle path used by a lot of our buyers: ship in bulk bags to keep the cubic volume down, then repack into jars or boxes locally for retail. You pay local labour instead of freighting air across an ocean.

One customs note if you repack locally: packaging can affect how goods are declared. The Harmonized System assigns six-digit codes internationally, extended to ten digits in the United States, and a retail-ready set can classify differently from the same components shipped loose. Worth a five-minute conversation with your broker before you commit a format for a large order.

4. Private label makes any format retail-ready

Every format above accepts branding: printed wrap labels on bottles and jars, sticker seals on bags, full print on boxes. Label content requirements (safety wording, weights, barcodes) are covered in the labeling guide, and the private label page shows real examples. Sustainability note: PET bottles and jars are recyclable and paper boxes compost — worth a line on your listing.

FAQ

Questions buyers ask about this topic

Which packaging is cheapest to start with?

Labeled OPP or ziplock bags — lowest unit cost, lightest freight, fastest filling. The trade-off is shelf presence; bags suit online refill listings more than boutique shelves.

Why did my freight quote double when I switched to bottles?

Volumetric weight. Bottles and jars ship mostly air: the same product volume can take 2–3× the carton space of bags, and sea/air freight charges by whichever is greater — actual or volumetric weight.

Can I mix packaging formats in one order?

Yes, and most brands should: bulk bags for your own production plus retail bottles or jars for shelf SKUs. One production run, two packing lines.

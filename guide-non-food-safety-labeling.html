Quality & Safety

Non-Food Safety Labeling for Craft Supplies

Updated July 6, 2026 · by the Qula Craft sales team

Quick answer: Every retail unit of fake sprinkles, clay slices, resin charms or craft fillers should carry at minimum: “Decorative craft material — not edible” and “Small parts — choking hazard. Not for children under 3.” If your product photographs like real candy, add the food-imitation line below. Printing these from day one is far cheaper than a marketplace takedown.

1. The minimum label — copy-paste templates

Wording you can lift directly onto your label artwork (adapt brand and weights):

ElementTemplate wording
Non-food“Decorative craft material. NOT EDIBLE — do not consume.”
Choking“⚠ WARNING: Small parts — choking hazard. Not suitable for children under 3 years. Use under adult supervision.”
Food-imitation“This product imitates food in appearance but is not food. Keep away from young children and food preparation areas.”
Material“Made of polymer clay / cured resin / acrylic. Decorative use only.”
TraceabilityBatch code + net weight + your brand’s contact (and importer address for EU/UK)

2. Why fake sprinkles are a special case

Most craft supplies don’t look edible — these do, by design. Products that imitate food are treated more strictly in several markets (the EU’s food-imitating product rules are the best-known example), because young children judge edibility by appearance. Practical consequences for your listings and packaging:

  • Don’t sell decorative sprinkles in packaging that mimics real food jars or candy bags without clear warnings on the front.
  • Keep “fake / decorative / not edible” wording in the product title or first line of marketplace listings — it also filters out mistaken food buyers before they leave a 1-star review.
  • For slime kits marketed to kids, warnings belong on the outer kit box, not only on inner bags.

3. Kids’ channels: what gets requested

If your product is sold as a toy or bundled into kids’ kits, buyers and platforms request third-party test reports — EN 71 (parts 1–3) for the EU, ASTM F963 for the US. We hold current batch reports for both, plus CPC, CPSC-CH-E1002, REACH, CE and RoHS, issued per product batch. Mention your target market and channel in the first inquiry so testing lead time is planned in — see our quality & safety process for how batch QC and documentation are handled.

3b. Why these look like food and are not

The labelling problem exists because the products are deliberately convincing. A clay slice is PVC-based polymer clay cured at roughly 129–135 °C; a jelly-bean charm is cast resin, a process used for collectible models and small-scale figures. Neither is food, neither is edible, and both are convincing enough at a glance that a small child could be confused.

That is the whole reason the non-edible wording is not optional decoration on the pack — it is the control that sits between a realistic-looking decorative object and a household with young children in it.

4. A 60-second pre-print checklist

  • Non-food line present and readable (not 4pt on the back).
  • Choking warning with age note where kids can access the product.
  • Food-imitation line for realistic candy/fruit designs.
  • Net weight matches actual fill (spot-check 10 units).
  • Batch code printed — makes any complaint traceable to one production run.
  • Your contact + barcode scannable on a curved bottle surface.

FAQ

Questions buyers ask about this topic

Do decorative sprinkles legally need a warning label?

Consumer product rules in most markets require truthful labeling and hazard warnings appropriate to foreseeable use. Because fake sprinkles look edible and reach children, non-food and choking warnings are the industry baseline — and marketplaces increasingly enforce them.

What testing do buyers in kids’ channels usually request?

Toy-adjacent channels request EN 71 (EU) or ASTM F963 (US) test reports. We hold batch reports for EN 71, ASTM F963, CPC, CPSC-CH-E1002 and REACH — tell us your market in the first inquiry and current reports for your product are included in the schedule.

Whose name goes on the label?

The brand owner’s (yours), plus an EU/UK responsible person address where applicable for those markets. As the manufacturer we support with batch codes and material declarations.
